SERVICE CONTROL POLICIES (SCPs) — OVERVIEW:
Service Control Policies are a type of organization policy that you can use to manage
permissions in your organization. SCPs offer central control over the maximum available
permissions for all accounts in your organization.

KEY EVALUATION RULES:
1. SCPs do not grant permissions. They define the maximum permissions that identity-based
   policies can grant to IAM principals (users and roles) in member accounts.
2. An effective permission requires BOTH: (a) SCP Allow (or no SCP deny in deny-list strategy),
   AND (b) IAM identity-based policy Allow.
3. An explicit SCP Deny overrides any IAM Allow — it is always effective regardless of what
   IAM identity policies permit.
4. The management account of an organization is NOT restricted by SCPs. SCPs apply only
   to member accounts.
5. Service-linked roles are exempt from SCP restrictions.

SCP STRATEGY TYPES:
- Deny-list strategy (default with FullAWSAccess): All actions allowed unless explicitly
  denied by a custom SCP. FullAWSAccess is a managed SCP allowing all actions. Custom SCPs
  add specific denies on top.
- Allow-list strategy: FullAWSAccess is removed; only explicitly allowed actions are
  permitted. Less common, more restrictive, more complex to manage.

SCPS AND PRIVILEGE ESCALATION PATHS:
In the context of IAM privilege escalation analysis:
- An SCP denying iam:AttachRolePolicy, iam:PutRolePolicy, or iam:CreatePolicyVersion
  organization-wide eliminates entire classes of escalation paths, even if the IAM
  identity policy permits them.
- SCPs denying sts:AssumeRole for certain conditions can block lateral movement paths.
- SCP protection is a defense-in-depth control: it does not replace IAM least privilege
  but provides an additional boundary.
- Removing an SCP would immediately reactivate all previously blocked escalation paths.

FRAMEWORK INTEGRATION:
Phase 2 of this framework applies SCP-aware filtering via scp_filter.py after BFS
path discovery. Paths blocked by SCPs are annotated as scp_blocked=True (latent risk)
rather than removed, reflecting that the IAM misconfiguration persists even when an SCP
provides temporary mitigation. This distinction is critical for risk management.

IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS:
- SCPs do not restrict the AWS account root user.
- SCPs cannot be applied to the management account.
- Resource-based policies are not controlled by SCPs.
- Permissions boundaries are separate from SCPs (both can restrict actions independently).
